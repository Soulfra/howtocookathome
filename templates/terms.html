Your Data

What we collect

Your restaurant name, subdomain, ingredient list, and optionally your email. That's it. No cookies. No third-party tracking pixels. No selling your data to anyone.

What we do with it

We match your ingredients against the USDA FoodData Central database (public domain, maintained by the U.S. Department of Agriculture). We generate your site, your nutrition labels, and your cookbook. Your brand config is stored as a YAML file — a plain text file you could open in any text editor.

What you own

Your recipes, your brand, your content — all yours. If you leave, you take it with you. We can export your data as YAML files or a PDF cookbook at any time. We don't claim ownership of anything you create on the platform.

What we own

The platform code, the USDA integration, the site templates, and the HTCAH brand. You're licensing use of the platform, not buying it.

In kitchen terms: You own your recipes. We own the kitchen equipment. If you move out, your recipes go with you — we don't keep a copy of grandma's secret sauce. The USDA data is public domain — it belongs to everyone (your tax dollars at work).

Your Token

When you sign up, you get a token — a unique string of characters. This is your key. No passwords, no login pages, no "forgot my password" flows. You present the token, you get access.

If you lose your token and provided an email during signup, we can help you recover it. If you didn't provide an email and lose your token, we cannot verify you're the owner. Keep it safe.

In kitchen terms: Your token is like the key to the restaurant. There's one copy. We don't keep a spare behind the register. If you gave us your email, we can cut you a new one. If not, locksmith can't help.

USDA Nutrition Data

All nutrition information comes from the USDA FoodData Central database. This data is public domain and provided as-is by the U.S. government. We match your ingredients to the closest USDA entry, but we are not a nutrition labeling service.

If you're using this data on actual food packaging or printed menus for regulatory compliance, you should verify it with a registered dietitian or use an FDA-compliant labeling service. Our data is accurate for education, planning, and general reference — not for FDA Nutrition Facts panel compliance.

In kitchen terms: We give you the calorie counts from the USDA's own books — same source the government uses. But if you're printing nutrition labels for retail packaging, get a dietitian to sign off. We're the prep cook, not the health inspector.

UPC Barcode Scanner

The consumer barcode scanner uses USDA Branded Foods data — nearly 2 million packaged products with UPC codes. Scanning a product shows you nutrition data sourced from manufacturer-reported information in the USDA database.

This data may not reflect recent reformulations. Always check the actual product label if you have specific dietary needs or allergies.
